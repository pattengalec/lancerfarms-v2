About the software

What is Grush?

The word at the bottom of every page on this site. Here is what it means.

Grush is a management platform for growing spaces, written by Chad Pattengale. It began as a way to run a farm he wanted to build — the beds, the plantings, the irrigation, the work log, the people doing the work — and grew into a system that turns the daily work of running a growing space into something simple enough to use with dirt on your hands.

It is privately developed. No investors, no committee. Every decision in it was made by someone who had to go out and do the work it describes.

Why it is on a garden website

Lancer Farms & Gardens is the first place Grush went to work. The garden needed two things that pull in opposite directions, and this site is the result of giving each one its own door.

The public side

So anyone can see the garden — what is growing, what the season is doing, how the work is done. Free, no account, nothing to sign.

The staff side

So students and crew can actually run the place — logging work, tracking supplies, approving photos, checking beds. This is the Grush part.

Same farm, same site, one shared record underneath. A visitor never needs an account. A student never needs a separate system. The feature guide walks through everything on both sides.
