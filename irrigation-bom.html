Supply & system settings

Design decisions

What this spec calls for and why — so anyone reading the BOM can follow the reasoning.

Pressure regulator — per zone

Campus mains run 60–80 PSI. Drip emitters want 25 PSI. Without a regulator, excess pressure blows ¼" tubing off barbs and shortens emitter life to weeks. One regulator per zone valve covers all beds on that station.

Filter — per zone, before the regulator

Silt and debris close pressure-compensating emitters irreversibly. A 155-mesh filter installs upstream of the regulator so it catches particles before they reach any emitter. It also goes before the regulator so debris doesn't grind across the regulator seat.

Pressure-compensating inline dripline

Fixed-orifice emitters lose flow as pressure drops along a run. PC emitters hold a consistent flow rate across a pressure range — typically 7 to 45 PSI — so every emitter on every bed delivers the same amount regardless of its position on the line.

Header tube per bed

A short section of ½" poly tubing runs along one end of the bed and feeds the rows of dripline via barb tees. This lets you unplug and replace a single row without disturbing anything else, and it keeps all connections accessible above the soil surface.

Emitter spacing: 12 inches

In a mixed teaching garden, spacing for the shallowest root system you grow. At 12", a 0.4 GPH emitter keeps surface soil uniformly moist for lettuce and transplants. Deeper-rooted crops tolerate the same pattern. Wider spacing saves tubing but creates dry zones for shallow work.

Path crossing — trip hazard

If the supply riser sits at the path end of a bed, any crossbar transecting the path needs to go under it in a sleeve — at minimum 1" PVC conduit, buried or flush with grade. Above-grade pipe on a student path is a safety violation. Budget a sleeve per crossing that crosses a walking surface.

Goof plugs — not optional

Every cut of dripline risks a misplaced start hole. Goof plugs seal ⅛" holes in ½" tubing and are effectively free at $0.10 each. Buy 50 per installation and keep them in the shed. The alternative is replacing a whole run because of one mistake.

UV-resistant materials throughout

The existing tubing failed because standard poly tubing isn't UV-rated. Every component in this spec is rated for direct sun exposure — black poly tubing, UV-stabilized emitter bodies. Any replacement tubing must carry a UV rating, or the same failure repeats in 18–24 months.
